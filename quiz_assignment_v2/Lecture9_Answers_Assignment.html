Q1. Per Vaswani et al.'s Table 1, the maximum path length between two tokens is \(O(1)\) for self-attention and ____ for a recurrent layer.

  • A. \(O(\log n)\)
  • B. \(O(n^2)\)
  • C. \(O(n)\)
  • D. \(O(1)\)
Answer C. An RNN passes information one step at a time; attention links every pair directly.

Q2. The price of self-attention's direct links is a per-layer cost that grows as:

  • A. \(n^2d\) in the sequence length \(n\)
  • B. \(\log n\)
  • C. \(d^3\)
  • D. It does not grow with \(n\)
Answer A. Every token scores every token: doubling the context quadruples the score matrix.

Q3. In the toy space, tiger \(-\) man \(+\) woman \(=(-1.00,0.05,0.95,0.00)\). Its nearest word by cosine (inputs excluded) is:

  • A. lioness
  • B. tigress
  • C. female
  • D. queen
Answer B. Cosines: tigress 0.991, lioness 0.967, female 0.789.

Q4. The sinusoidal encoding uses frequencies \(\omega_i=10000^{-2i/d_{\text{model}}}\). With \(d_{\text{model}}=4\), the second pair has \(\omega_1=\)

  • A. 0.1
  • B. 0.0001
  • C. 1
  • D. 0.01
Answer D. \(10000^{-2/4}=10000^{-1/2}=0.01\).

Q5. For \(d_{\text{model}}=4\), the positional encoding of position 1 is about:

  • A. \((0.8415,0.5403,0.0100,1.0000)\)
  • B. \((1,1,1,1)\)
  • C. \((0,1,0,1)\)
  • D. \((0.5403,0.8415,1.0000,0.0100)\)
Answer A. \((\sin1,\cos1,\sin0.01,\cos0.01)\). Position 0 is \((0,1,0,1)\).

Q6. Why does the paper say sinusoids let the model attend by relative position?

  • A. They are learned
  • B. They are concatenated to the embedding
  • C. \(PE_{pos+k}\) is a fixed rotation (linear map) of \(PE_{pos}\) that depends only on \(k\)
  • D. They are always positive
Answer C. The angle-addition formulas give a rotation matrix in \(\omega k\) alone.

Q7. In the worked example, \(q_{\text{enemy}}=(2,0)\) and \(k_{\text{tank}}=(2,0)\). The scaled score with \(d_k=2\) is:

  • A. 4
  • B. 2.8284
  • C. 1.4142
  • D. 2
Answer B. \(4/\sqrt2=2.8284\).

Q8. Row enemy has scaled scores \((0,2.8284,1.4142)\). The attention weight on tank is about:

  • A. 0.768
  • B. 0.333
  • C. 0.867
  • D. 0.187
Answer A. \(16.9188/(1+16.9188+4.1133)=16.9188/22.0321=0.7679\).

Q9. If query and key components are independent with mean 0 and variance 1, the dot product \(q\cdot k\) has variance:

  • A. 1
  • B. \(\sqrt{d_k}\)
  • C. \(1/d_k\)
  • D. \(d_k\)
Answer D. A sum of \(d_k\) unit-variance terms; hence the division by \(\sqrt{d_k}\).

Q10. With the causal mask, the row for “tank” (scaled scores 1.4142 and 0 on enemy and tank) gets weights:

  • A. \((0.576,0.140,0.284)\)
  • B. \((0.804,0.196,0)\)
  • C. \((0.5,0.5,0)\)
  • D. \((1,0,0)\)
Answer B. The future token is set to \(-\infty\); renormalising \((4.113,1)\) gives 0.804 and 0.196.

Q11. In the base transformer, \(h=8\) heads with \(d_k=d_v=64\) are concatenated into a vector of width:

  • A. 64
  • B. 8
  • C. 512
  • D. 2048
Answer C. \(8\times64=512=d_{\text{model}}\); \(W^O\) is \(512\times512\).

Q12. How many parameters (with biases) does one multi-head attention layer of the base model have?

  • A. \(4\times512^2+4\times512=1{,}050{,}624\)
  • B. \(8\times64\times64=32{,}768\)
  • C. \(2\times512\times2048=2{,}097{,}152\)
  • D. It depends on the number of heads
Answer A. \(W^Q,W^K,W^V,W^O\) are each \(512\times512\) in total, independent of \(h\).

Q13. In encoder–decoder (cross) attention, block 5 of Figure 1, no mask is needed because:

  • A. The decoder never sees the encoder
  • B. Softmax ignores future words
  • C. Queries come from the encoder
  • D. The whole source sentence is known before decoding starts
Answer D. Keys and values come from the encoder's output; only the target side has a “future”.

Q14. “Outputs (shifted right)” in Figure 1 means that during training the decoder input is:

  • A. The correct target sentence moved one place right behind a start token
  • B. The source sentence reversed
  • C. Random tokens
  • D. The encoder output
Answer A. Position \(t\) predicts word \(t\) while seeing only words before it (teacher forcing).

Q15. A defence team needs to tag units, places and equipment in intercepted text. The most natural model family is:

  • A. Decoder-only (GPT-style)
  • B. Encoder–decoder for translation
  • C. Encoder-only (BERT-style), using bidirectional context
  • D. A recurrent network
Answer C. Tagging is understanding, not generation; bidirectional context helps each token's label.

Part B — Assignment

Instructions. Five problems, 100 marks, plus a 20-mark bonus problem (Problem 6). Reference: Lecture 9 reference text.

Problem 1: Sinusoidal positional encoding 20 marks

Take $d_{\text{model}}=6$.

(a) Compute the three frequencies $\omega_i=10000^{-2i/6}$. [3]
(b) Compute $PE_0,PE_1,PE_2,PE_3$ to four decimals. [8]
(c) Verify that $PE_1\cdot PE_3=PE_2\cdot PE_4$ by showing both equal $\sum_i\cos(2\omega_i)$, and evaluate it. [5]
(d) Why is $p_t=t$ a poor position code, and why might sinusoids extrapolate to longer inputs than learned vectors? [4]

Show full solution

(a) $\omega_0=1$, $\omega_1=10000^{-1/3}=0.046416$, $\omega_2=10000^{-2/3}=0.0021544$.

(b) $PE_t=(\sin t,\cos t,\sin\omega_1t,\cos\omega_1t,\sin\omega_2t,\cos\omega_2t)$: $PE_0=(0,1,0,1,0,1)$; $PE_1=(0.8415,0.5403,0.0464,0.9989,0.0022,1.0000)$; $PE_2=(0.9093,-0.4161,0.0927,0.9957,0.0043,1.0000)$; $PE_3=(0.1411,-0.9900,0.1388,0.9903,0.0065,1.0000)$.

(c) For each pair, $\sin a\sin b+\cos a\cos b=\cos(a-b)$, so $PE_p\cdot PE_{p+k}=\sum_i\cos(\omega_ik)$, which depends only on $k=2$: $\cos2+\cos0.0928+\cos0.0043=-0.4161+0.9957+1.0000=\mathbf{1.5795}$ for both.

(d) $p_t=t$ is unbounded: at position 1,000 it would swamp the meaning in the embedding, and its scale depends on length. Sinusoids are bounded in $[-1,1]$ and defined for any position by the formula, with relative shifts as fixed rotations, whereas learned vectors exist only for positions seen in training.

Problem 2: Scaled dot-product attention by hand 20 marks

Three tokens with $X=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\0&1&1\\1&0&1\end{pmatrix}$, $W^Q=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}$, $W^K=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$, $W^V=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}$, $d_k=2$.

(a) Compute $Q$, $K$ and $V$. [5]
(b) Compute $S=QK^{\mathsf T}$ and $S/\sqrt2$. [5]
(c) Compute the attention weights $A$ (four decimals). [6]
(d) Compute $Z=AV$. [4]

Show full solution

(a) $Q=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&2\\2&1\end{pmatrix}$, $K=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\1&1\\2&1\end{pmatrix}$, $V=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\\3&1\end{pmatrix}$.

(b) $S=\begin{pmatrix}3&2&3\\5&3&4\\4&3&5\end{pmatrix}$; $S/\sqrt2=\begin{pmatrix}2.1213&1.4142&2.1213\\3.5355&2.1213&2.8284\\2.8284&2.1213&3.5355\end{pmatrix}$.

(c) Row 1: exponentials $(8.3421,4.1133,8.3421)$, sum $20.7975$: $(0.4011,0.1978,0.4011)$. Row 2: $(34.3133,8.3421,16.9188)$, sum $59.5742$: $(0.5760,0.1400,0.2840)$. Row 3: $(16.9188,8.3421,34.3133)$: $(0.2840,0.1400,0.5760)$.

(d) $Z=\begin{pmatrix}2.2033&1.1978\\2.1440&1.1400\\2.4359&1.1400\end{pmatrix}$. E.g. row 3: $0.284(2,1)+0.14(1,2)+0.576(3,1)=(2.4359,1.1400)$.

Problem 3: Multi-head shapes and parameter counts 20 marks

BERT-base: $d_{\text{model}}=768$, $h=12$, feed-forward width 3,072, input of $n=128$ tokens.

(a) Give $d_k$ and the shapes of $Q_i$, $K_i$, $V_i$, the score matrix of one head, the concatenation and the output. [6]
(b) How many attention scores are computed per layer? [3]
(c) Count the parameters of the attention sub-layer and of the feed-forward sub-layer. [7]
(d) Why must the output have the same width as the input? [4]

Show full solution

(a) $d_k=768/12=64$. $Q_i,K_i,V_i$: $128\times64$; scores per head $128\times128$; concatenation $128\times768$; after $W^O$ ($768\times768$): $128\times768$.

(b) $12\times128\times128=\mathbf{196{,}608}$.

(c) Attention: $4\times768^2+4\times768=\mathbf{2{,}362{,}368}$. Feed-forward: $2\times768\times3{,}072+3{,}072+768=\mathbf{4{,}722{,}432}$. The feed-forward layer holds two-thirds of the block.

(d) So the output can be added to the input in the residual connection and so identical blocks can be stacked.

Problem 4: Masking and cost 20 marks

(a) Apply the causal mask to the scaled scores of Problem 2 and recompute $A$ and $Z$. Which row is unchanged? [8]
(b) By what factor does the number of attention scores grow when the context goes from 512 to 2,048 tokens? [4]
(c) A model has 16 heads and stores scores in 2-byte numbers. How much memory do one layer's score matrices need at 4,096 tokens? [4]
(d) Where in Figure 1 is the mask used, and where is it not, and why? [4]

Show full solution

(a) Row 1: $(1,0,0)$, $z_1=v_1=(2,1)$. Row 2: softmax of $(3.5355,2.1213)$: $(0.8044,0.1956,0)$, $z_2=(1.8044,1.1956)$. Row 3 sees everything and is unchanged: $(0.2840,0.1400,0.5760)$, $z_3=(2.4359,1.1400)$.

(b) $(2048/512)^2=\mathbf{16}$ times.

(c) $4{,}096^2\times16\times2=536{,}870{,}912$ bytes $=\mathbf{512}$ MiB per layer, before any other activations: why long contexts are expensive.

(d) Used in the decoder's masked self-attention (block 4), so position $i$ cannot see later target words. Not used in encoder self-attention (the whole source is known) or in encoder–decoder attention (keys and values are the known source).

Problem 5: BERT, GPT or encoder–decoder for a defence task 20 marks

For each task choose a model family and justify it: (a) tagging units, places and equipment in intercepted messages; (b) drafting a SITREP from field notes; (c) translating captured documents. [12]
(d) For task (b), name two failure modes and a safeguard for each. [8]

Show full solution

(a) Encoder-only (BERT-type). Tagging is understanding: each token's label benefits from context on both sides, which the unmasked encoder provides; a small classification head sits on each token's vector.

(b) Decoder-only (GPT-type), or an encoder–decoder. Drafting is generation; a decoder trained to predict the next token, given the notes as a prompt, writes the report one token at a time.

(c) Encoder–decoder, the original transformer's task: the encoder reads the source language bidirectionally; the decoder writes the target, consulting the source through cross-attention.

(d) Hallucination: fluent invented details; safeguard: require each statement to cite its source note and have an officer verify before release. Prompt injection or adversarial text in the notes: safeguard: treat inputs as data, restrict output to the report template, and log inputs for audit. (Also acceptable: distribution shift to unfamiliar jargon, with fine-tuning and testing on unit data.)

Problem 6 (bonus): Add & Norm by hand 20 marks

(a) A token's input is $x=(2,0,1,1)$ and its sub-layer output is $(0.5,1.5,-0.5,0.5)$. Compute $h=x+\text{Sublayer}(x)$, its mean, variance and standard deviation, and $\text{LN}(h)$ with $\gamma=1$, $\beta=0$. [8]
(b) Recompute with $\gamma=(1,1,2,2)$ and $\beta=(0,0,0.5,0.5)$. [4]
(c) Repeat (a) for $x=(1,3,0,2)$ and sub-layer output $(1,1,0,-2)$. [5]
(d) Why does a transformer normalise each token separately rather than over the batch? [3]

Show full solution

(a) $h=(2.5,1.5,0.5,1.5)$; mean $1.5$; deviations $(1,0,-1,0)$; variance $0.5$; s.d. $0.7071$. $\text{LN}(h)=(\mathbf{1.4142},\ \mathbf{0},\ \mathbf{-1.4142},\ \mathbf{0})$.

(b) $\gamma\odot\text{LN}(h)+\beta=(1.4142,\ 0,\ -2.8284+0.5,\ 0+0.5)=(1.4142,\ 0,\ -2.3284,\ 0.5)$.

(c) $h=(2,4,0,0)$; mean $1.5$; deviations $(0.5,2.5,-1.5,-1.5)$; variance $(0.25+6.25+2.25+2.25)/4=2.75$; s.d. $1.6583$. $\text{LN}(h)=(0.3015,\ 1.5076,\ -0.9045,\ -0.9045)$.

(d) Sequences vary in length and padding, and at inference a single sequence may be processed alone; per-token statistics do not depend on the other examples in the batch, so training and inference behave identically.
